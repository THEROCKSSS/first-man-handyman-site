## What the site really does (so the privacy policy is true)

Checked 2026-11-04.

**The current live site** (firstmanhandyman.com) runs on **WordPress with the Astra theme and Elementor**. A normal page load makes requests only to its own server plus WordPress emoji images from `s.w.org`. It sets no cookies, has no forms, no analytics scripts, no embedded maps or videos, and no Terms or Privacy link. The hosting company is not visible from the page.

**These designs** are a static website: no backend, no database, no accounts, no forms that send data, no cookies, no analytics, no third-party fonts or images (fonts are self-hosted). The only thing stored is the "text helper" remembering the visitor's town and chosen job types **in their own browser** (`localStorage`, key `fmh-text-helper`), never the free-text note. Buttons open the visitor's own text or email app with a message they can edit. Outbound links go to Google, Facebook and Instagram.

If this ever changes (a booking form, an online payment, a chat, analytics, an email list, a map), the privacy policy **must be updated before launch**.

## Questions for Adam (things only he or a professional can answer)

- **Contractor license.** California requires a license for most jobs over a small dollar limit (labor plus materials). The limit has been $500 and may have been raised, so check the current figure with the Contractors State License Board (CSLB). If he is licensed, the license number usually has to appear in his advertising; if he works only under the limit, ask what the rules say about how he describes the work. The site currently states neither.
- **Insurance and warranty.** The terms say nothing about either on purpose. Add only what he really offers.
- **Estimates, deposits and payment.** The terms say estimates are not final until both sides agree. If he takes deposits or has a cancellation rule, add it in his words.
- **Reviews.** The names shown are from public Google reviews. Confirm he is comfortable showing them.
- **Customer photos.** Photos show customers' homes. Confirm each customer is fine with it.
- **Hosting logs.** Whoever hosts the final site may keep standard server logs. The policy says so in general terms; if he picks a host, check what it logs.
- **California privacy law.** The policy gives people a simple way to ask what he holds and to ask for deletion. Whether the formal law applies to a business his size is a question for a professional.
